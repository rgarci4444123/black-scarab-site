## Black Scarab application

### Editorial purpose

Build a global audience for research on robotics, autonomy, and industrial AI. Connect technology to operating performance, buying decisions, and commercial durability. Mexico may be relevant to a particular story or service, but is not the boundary of the editorial audience.

Write as an informed analyst explaining a consequential system to an intelligent reader. Make a clear argument and show the evidence. Use Black Scarab's own language, examples, analysis, and art. Do not copy another publisher's prose, distinctive marks, chart assets, or investment positioning. Do not imitate an individual author's personal voice.

### Article construction

1. Title: company or subject plus the real question. Prefer clarity to a string of SEO keywords.
2. Opening: establish the operating problem and why the company matters now within two short paragraphs.
3. Reading map: identify three or four questions the article will resolve.
4. Company and product: explain what is sold, to whom, and where the system fits.
5. Technology: follow the physical workflow through sensing, computing, control, and output. Introduce terms before abbreviations. Explain the practical consequence of each important component choice.
6. Hardware and suppliers: name only supported products and relationships, with source dates and compatibility limits.
7. Commercial evidence: distinguish research programs, pilots, integrations, signed customers, and routine production. Never treat these as interchangeable.
8. Economics: discuss pricing evidence, the complete installed cost, utilization, supervision, and accepted output. Clearly label every fictional example.
9. Alternatives and limitations: give the strongest credible competing approach and the unresolved technical or commercial questions.
10. Verdict: state who should evaluate the system, what would make it attractive, and which observable evidence would change the view.
11. Sources and method: include primary source links and a short provenance note. Apply the external link policy below. Author: Rodolfo Garcia Calderoni, CFA.

Choose length according to substance. A focused X adaptation can be roughly 1,400 to 2,200 words; a comprehensive original deep dive can be longer. This range is a Black Scarab production choice, not a measured RoboStrategy rule. Avoid forcing all website material into one cramped post.

### Language and pacing

Use mostly one to three sentences per paragraph. Follow an assertion with its reason, evidence, or implication. Allow an occasional short sentence to emphasize a meaningful conclusion. Avoid repetitive fragments, rhetorical questions in every paragraph, generic futurism, exaggerated certainty, and marketing adjectives.

Explain technical concepts with physical examples: a misplaced part, an occluded camera, an intervention, a failed acceptance test. Carry one example through several sections when it helps. A comparison should explain an engineering or commercial tradeoff, not merely list brand names.

No dash punctuation in published headlines, prose, captions, or graphic labels. Hyphens that are part of URLs and exact technical identifiers may remain. Attribute company claims, forecasts, and reported customer outcomes. Never invent prices, processors, control rates, customers, or measured performance. Do not add unrelated NVIDIA references.

### Visual system

Keep the existing scarab as Black Scarab's identifying mark. Use generous clear space and modest logo scale. Do not adopt RoboStrategy's brackets or copy its banner composition.

Use two complementary visual surfaces:

* Covers: a single relevant subject, controlled contrast, generous negative space, a concise editorial headline, restrained byline or series label, and small Black Scarab identification. Dark charcoal may be used for research covers, with one subdued color detail.
* Analytical figures: white or near white, dark text, thin rules, and one muted accent used to guide reading. Favor diagrams and compact comparisons over decorative collages. Use original editable graphics for exact labels and data.

Retain the user's approved white avatar, white banner with a faint blue lower edge, and white peach Insights introduction until a replacement is specifically requested. ### Images inside articles

Plan images with the outline. Each image should answer a question in its neighboring paragraphs. For a focused adaptation, start with one cover and three or four purposeful inline figures; increase only when useful. This is our working target, not a rigid reference cadence.

Useful sequence: system diagram near architecture, supplier or compatibility table near hardware, evidence timeline near commercial progress, and sensitivity chart near economics. Place the explanation before the figure and interpretation after it. Avoid several unrelated images in a row.

Every figure needs a clear title, readable labels, an explanatory caption, and a source or method note. A hypothetical chart must say so within the image, not only in the article. A functional diagram must not imply a proprietary or certified schematic. Preserve meaningful product colors in genuine photos when they identify components. Generated images must say editorial illustration and must never stand in for evidence of a real product or deployment.

Compose for mobile. Inspect graphics at approximately 360 CSS pixels wide. Split dense tables rather than shrinking labels. Export clean PNGs for text and diagrams. Use the dimensions accepted by the current X editor and inspect its actual crop before publishing. Do not upscale a small source and claim improved detail.
