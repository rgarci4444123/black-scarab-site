# Black Scarab editorial image direction

Approved September 24, 2026.

Black Scarab should look coherent as a publication without making every story look like a variation of the same image. The interface, typography, editorial judgment, captions, and quality bar carry the identity. Editorial artwork is free to change color, medium, density, perspective, scale, and mood.

## The visual thesis comes first

Begin with one sentence describing what the cover should communicate, not merely what objects it should contain. A robotics investment story might visualize industrial capacity being built, the compression of supply chains, or the scale difference between a component and a network. It does not automatically require a robot in a factory.

Choose the concept from the reporting. Do not use an abstract metaphor if it could be mistaken for evidence, a real facility, a real product, or a disclosed technical design.

## Creative range

Choose deliberately across every dimension below. The lists are starting points, not a menu that limits experimentation.

| Dimension | Possible directions |
| --- | --- |
| Medium | documentary realism, cinematic realism, analog collage, paper construction, technical cutaway, architectural visualization, isometric miniature, graphic silhouette, screen print, archival halftone, painterly concept, macro material study, abstract data landscape |
| Composition | extreme close view, overhead grid, wide environment, asymmetric field, cross section, exploded assembly, diptych, triptych, centered icon, route or map, layered depth, sequential motion |
| Subject scale | material, component, end effector, machine, workcell, building, campus, logistics network, landscape, human and machine interaction |
| Viewpoint | ground level, low angle, eye level, elevated oblique, top down, aerial, inside the machine, through glass, behind the operator, orthographic |
| Palette | unrestricted; use any colors, combination, saturation, or tonal range that serves the story and the complete visual concept |
| Light | hard flash, flat editorial, overcast daylight, dawn, dusk, backlit silhouette, fluorescent industrial, top light, projected light, practical workshop light |
| Surface | polished, dusty, oily, brushed metal, translucent, paper grain, concrete, glass, fabric, raw composite, printed ink |

Color should respond to the story. There is no house palette for editorial artwork and no color family is preferred, required, discouraged, or prohibited. Ivory, charcoal, sage, orange, blue, and every other color remain equally available. Do not replace one default palette with another.

## Anti repetition rule

Before generating a new cover, inspect at least the six most recently published covers and note their visual fingerprints.

A visual fingerprint contains:

* medium
* composition
* primary subject and subject scale
* viewpoint
* dominant palette
* lighting
* surface or texture
* visual metaphor, if any

The new concept must differ from every recent cover on at least three dimensions. Evaluate repetition across the complete recipe rather than treating palette as a quota or restriction. A color family may recur whenever it fits the story, provided the overall concept does not feel recycled. Avoid reusing a centered robot, wide factory interior, glowing network lines, or any other familiar device simply because it worked before.

If the first result resembles a recent cover, revise the brief at the recipe level. Change the medium, viewpoint, scale, or composition instead of making minor color adjustments.

## Editorial guardrails

* Create original artwork or use a source with confirmed editorial rights.
* Generated imagery must be labeled as an editorial illustration or interpretation.
* Never present an invented product, facility, customer deployment, technical schematic, or supplier relationship as fact.
* Do not use company logos, marks, interface text, labels, numbers, or watermarks unless the concept requires verified typography and it is reviewed character by character.
* Do not mimic a living artist or another publication's signature style.
* Preserve enough focal clarity for both the wide website hero and the cropped news card, but do not force the subject into the same position on every cover.
* Write useful alternative text that describes the visible concept and does not overstate what is real.

## Portfolio rhythm

Judge the image as part of the full news grid, not only as an isolated cover. Across a run of covers, vary medium, composition, scale, viewpoint, lighting, texture, and visual thesis when the stories support it. Palette may vary or recur naturally. Never force a color change merely to satisfy a diversity count. Story relevance and the strength of the complete visual concept remain the deciding factors.
