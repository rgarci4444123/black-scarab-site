## Reporting record

Sharpa announced D01, W02, and AE01 at IROS 2026 in Pittsburgh on September 28. D01 is an integrated manipulation robot, W02 is a compact tactile dexterous hand, and AE01 is a haptic exoskeleton data glove for teleoperation and demonstration capture.

The product specifications in the report come from Sharpa's launch announcement and product pages. They are attributed as company specifications and have not been presented as independent benchmark results.

NVIDIA independently confirms that it selected Sharpa's earlier Wave hand for the Isaac GR00T reference humanoid built on a Unitree H2 Plus body. The Associated Press independently reported Sharpa's role in that reference design.

DealStreetAsia reported Sharpa's financing, company background, Singapore headquarters, Shanghai manufacturing and research operation, California business operation, and Dairy Queen restaurant deployment. Operational claims about the restaurant largely originate with Sharpa. No public orders per hour, intervention rate, uptime, waste, staffing, service cost, or customer economics were found.

Sharpa's product pages accept quote requests but do not disclose list prices, standard configurations, warranties, lead times, delivery schedules, production capacity, service plans, or named D01 customers. Sharpa has not published an independent test protocol, complete safety certification, or endurance results for the new products.

NEWS | Sharpa

Sharpa has introduced a robot, a tactile hand, and a haptic data glove at IROS 2026.

The stronger story is how the three products fit together. AE01 captures human hand motion for teleoperation and training data. W02 gives robots a compact hand with sensing across the fingertips, fingers, and palm. D01 brings the hands, arms, onboard computing, and body contact sensing into one platform.

That creates a potential loop from human demonstration to robot execution, tactile feedback, correction, and another round of learning.

Sharpa publishes detailed specifications and already supplies the hands used in NVIDIA's Isaac GR00T reference humanoid. The new products still need independent benchmarks, customer deployments, pricing, delivery schedules, and evidence that the integrated stack can perform reliably through real shifts.

Our report examines the hardware, the training data strategy, Sharpa's commercial evidence, and what the IROS launch does not yet prove.
