# Axon expands its Skydio stake and public safety sales partnership

By Rodolfo Garcia Calderoni, CFA

Axon’s roughly $1.2 billion staged investment is expected to lift its Skydio stake to about 37%, alongside exclusive public safety distribution rights in the United States and Canada.

## A bigger stake and a closer operating relationship

Axon announced an additional investment of approximately $1.2 billion in Skydio on November 5, alongside an exclusive public safety distribution agreement for the United States and Canada.

The staged investment is expected to increase Axon’s ownership from about 15% at September 30 to approximately 37% on a fully diluted basis. An initial $254.9 million primary purchase has closed, with the remaining transactions expected during the fourth quarter of 2026 and first quarter of 2027.

Skydio builds autonomous drones that use onboard perception to navigate and avoid obstacles, with visible and thermal imaging for emergency response, inspection and other field work. Axon’s drone operations platform connects aircraft, remote operators and live video with the systems agencies use to coordinate a response and preserve evidence.

The deal gives Axon a larger financial interest in Skydio’s development and a stronger position in the customer relationship around each deployment.

## Follow the money carefully

The total consideration is expected to be about 60% cash, or $720 million, with the remainder in Axon shares. The final combination depends on settlement of the transactions.

The November 5 primary closing involved approximately $254.9 million for newly issued, nonvoting Skydio Series G preferred shares. Axon’s quarterly filing makes the payment timing more precise: approximately $127.5 million was paid in cash, alongside a commitment to deliver approximately $127.5 million in Axon stock.

The remaining investments include a tender offer and secondary purchases. Those payments go to selling shareholders. Only the primary investment puts new capital directly into Skydio’s operating budget.

This distinction changes the economic reading. New capital can support product development and expansion. Secondary purchases rearrange ownership and give selling shareholders liquidity. Both may support a strategic partnership, but they do different jobs.

Axon describes the investment as a minority position. Its quarterly filing says the investments will remain within nonmarketable equity investments and that it does not have significant influence over Skydio’s operations. A large percentage interest should therefore not be casually treated as an acquisition of the manufacturer.

## Distribution is the strategic asset

The exclusivity has a defined boundary: public safety customers in the United States and Canada. It does not amount to worldwide exclusivity across every industry Skydio serves.

The service at the center of the agreement is Drone as First Responder, or DFR. Aircraft can launch from docks to give remote teams a view of an incident before responders arrive. Axon’s current product page connects outdoor coverage using Skydio’s X10 with live video, flight records and evidence management.

Within that boundary, the logic is straightforward. An aircraft can provide an early view of an emergency, but its commercial usefulness also depends on where the footage goes and how people use it. A supplier connected to agency workflows can sell more than the flying hardware.

Axon’s shareholder letter supplies an especially useful detail. As a direct channel partner, it recognizes the associated revenue on a net basis within Software & Services. Readers should not assume every dollar an agency spends on a Skydio system becomes a dollar of Axon revenue.

Black Scarab’s reading is that Axon is pursuing two different returns. The ownership stake provides exposure to Skydio’s broader development. The channel rights position Axon to earn from a specific customer relationship and make its wider platform more useful.

For Skydio, the arrangement offers a concentrated route into public safety while preserving a separate company serving other markets. The tradeoff is dependence on the execution of that channel. A strong distributor can reduce commercial friction, but exclusivity also makes coordination, service quality and priorities more consequential.

## The connection already reaches the evidence room

Skydio’s support documentation shows how practical that relationship already is. Flight images and video are recorded aboard the aircraft, uploaded to Skydio Cloud, and then transferred into Axon Evidence through an integration using the Axon Air API.

The process requires an agency administrator to configure the connection. Uploaded files also need an associated user. Skydio documents a default user setting for cases where a pilot’s email cannot be matched to the agency’s Axon organization.

These details help explain the commercial value of the integration. Agencies need footage attached to a usable record, with a workable path from capture to storage and review. The aircraft is only the beginning of that chain.

The same documentation describes file integrity checks and separate options to erase media from the aircraft or Skydio Cloud after a verified transfer. That highlights a continuing operational responsibility: expanding a drone program also expands the importance of account configuration, retention choices and evidence handling.

A functioning integration is part of the service agencies must actually operate.

## Scale is promising, but count the right things

Axon says Skydio serves more than 1,500 public safety customers. It also reports that deployed Skydio docks through Axon Air have more than doubled from a year earlier. Those are signs of an established commercial relationship rather than a partnership starting from zero.

Skydio expects approximately $1 billion in total bookings in its current fiscal year, according to the announcement. Bookings are a forward commercial indicator, not the same as recognized revenue or cash collected. The figure also concerns Skydio’s business, rather than a disclosed amount earned by Axon through this distribution agreement.

Public sector deployment timing remains a concrete constraint. In the same earnings release, Axon reduced its annual growth expectations because three large federal Dedrone deployments, representing approximately $130 million of revenue, were no longer expected in 2026.

Dedrone supplies airspace security systems, and these delayed projects are separate from the Skydio investment. They show how public sector deployment schedules can move revenue between periods even when a supplier reports strong demand. For the new arrangement, delivered systems and recognized channel revenue will be more concrete measures of progress than a bookings forecast alone.

## What comes next

The first milestone is completion of the remaining investment steps, including settlement of the tender offer and secondary purchases. That will clarify the final cash and stock mix and the resulting ownership position.

The next commercial signals are expansion of deployed docks, conversion of customer commitments into operating programs, and the contribution of the channel relationship to Axon’s Software & Services business. Those measures answer different questions and should remain separate.

The central bet is that distribution and workflow integration can make autonomous aircraft a repeatable public safety service. Axon has secured a stronger position at both the ownership and customer relationship levels. The test now is how effectively those positions translate into deployments that agencies keep using.
